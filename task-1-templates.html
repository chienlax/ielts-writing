IELTS Task 1 Skeleton Templates

Plug-and-play scaffolds for every chart type. Distilled from a Band 8+ model corpus. Replace the bracketed placeholders, keep the connective tissue.

00. Universal rules

These apply to every task type. Install them once, they carry across.

Macro structure

  1. One-sentence intro that paraphrases the prompt.
  2. One-paragraph overview opening with Overall,, containing at least two key features, no specific figures.
  3. Two or three body paragraphs, each grouped by a logical category.
  4. No conclusion. The essay ends on the final body paragraph's last sentence.

Body paragraph openers

Lead each body paragraph with a fronted adverbial, not the subject. Four types cover almost every case:

Time-fronted
In 1995, From 1979 to 1989, By 2050, Over the next five years,
Category-fronted
In terms of energy use, With exports, For Fairtrade bananas,
Contrast-fronted
In contrast, In comparison, Conversely,
Process-fronted
Prior to development, Following construction, At the end of this stage, Above ground,

The reusable moves

Participle clause
Compresses a follow-up clause into a modifier. ..., reaching 25 million by 2010. ..., overtaking computers to become the second most owned category.
Concessive structure
Acknowledges a counter-point inside one sentence. Despite [NP], [main clause]. Although [clause], [main clause]. [clause], albeit [adv/adj].
Past perfect for end state
Frames the closing figure as the outcome of a sequence. By 2010, X had become... By the end of the period, X had fallen to...
Respectively
Attaches two paired figures to two paired items in one go. ...reaching 80% and 70% respectively by 1999.
Notably / Interestingly
Flags the single most important wrinkle. One use per essay maximum.

The four-error checklist

Run these before submitting any essay:

  1. Every singular countable noun has an article, or is pluralised. washing machine → the washing machine or washing machines.
  2. Million following a numeral takes no -s. 25 millions → 25 million.
  3. with + noun takes -ing, not a past participle. with X recorded → with X recording.
  4. In the end → by the end of the period or by [year].
Banned

No em-dashes, anywhere. No In conclusion,. No Furthermore, or Moreover, (essentially absent from the model corpus). No bullet points inside the essay. No I think, or personal opinion. No restating the overview at the end. No spike for a sustained rise.

01. Line graph

Trends over time. Look for starting positions, ending positions, ranking shifts, peaks and troughs, rate-of-change differences, and crossovers between lines.

Skeleton

Intro

The line graph shows [DATA DESCRIPTION] in [PLACE] from [YEAR_START] to [YEAR_END].

Overview

Overall, [TREND DIRECTION] across all [N] categories during this period, although [PACE OR RANKING CONTRAST]. Notably, [HEADLINE FEATURE: crossover, surge, dip, etc.].

Body 1: top tier / starting state

In [YEAR_START], [LEADING CATEGORY] was already the most [ADJ] at [FIGURE], followed by [SECOND CATEGORY] at [FIGURE]. Both continued to [CLIMB / FALL / FLUCTUATE] steadily, reaching [FIGURE_A] and [FIGURE_B] respectively by [YEAR_END], with [LEADING CATEGORY] remaining the leader throughout.

Body 2: middle / contrasting group

In contrast, the remaining [N] categories began from a considerably lower base. [CATEGORY] started at approximately [FIGURE] and increased only [PACE] until [YEAR], before climbing sharply to around [FIGURE] by [YEAR_END], overtaking [CATEGORY] to become the [NEW RANK]. [OTHER CATEGORY] followed a similar but less dramatic path, rising from [FIGURE] to [FIGURE] over the same period.

Body 3: outlier (optional)

[CATEGORY] was the exception. Despite starting at [FIGURE] in [YEAR_START], the figure dropped to approximately [FIGURE] by [YEAR], before recovering steadily to [FIGURE] by [YEAR_END]. By this point, [CATEGORY] had become the [FINAL RANK].

Vocabulary kit

Direction verbs (up)
rose climbed increased grew gained surged soared
Direction verbs (down)
fell declined dropped dipped sank plummeted
Stable / mixed
remained stable levelled off stabilised fluctuated varied
Pace adverbs
sharply dramatically steeply steadily gradually modestly marginally slightly
Crossover language
overtook surpassed drew level with was overtaken by moved ahead of
Peak / trough
peaked at reached a peak of hit a trough at bottomed out at a low of
Recovery
before recovering to then rebounded to climbed back to
Pitfalls

Don't use spike for sustained rises (a spike is brief and returns to baseline). If the overall trend is upward but one segment dipped, frame it as registered an initial decline before recovering rather than only X declined. Whenever a clean crossover exists between two lines, it belongs in the overview, not buried in a body paragraph.

02. Bar chart

Bar charts come in two flavours. Time-based bar charts behave like line graphs, use the line graph skeleton. Categorical bar charts are snapshot comparisons across groups, use the skeleton below.

Skeleton categorical

Intro

The bar chart shows [DATA] across [CATEGORIES] in [PLACE / YEAR].

Overview

Overall, [DOMINANT PATTERN OR ABSENCE OF ONE], with [HIGHEST GROUP] [DESCRIPTION] and [LOWEST GROUP] [DESCRIPTION]. Notably, [HEADLINE OBSERVATION].

Body 1: first logical group

In [CATEGORY A] and [CATEGORY B], [SIMILAR PATTERN]. In [CATEGORY A], around [FIGURE] of [GROUP] were [PROPERTY]. In [CATEGORY B], slightly [MORE / FEWER] at [FIGURE]. However, [FURTHER DETAIL OR PEAK].

Body 2: contrasting group

[OTHER CATEGORIES] followed a similar pattern albeit in reverse. In both, [OBSERVATION], though [DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM]. In contrast, in [CATEGORY], [OPPOSITE PATTERN], again making up [FIGURE] of [GROUP]. Notably, the lowest figure occurred in [SPECIFIC SUBGROUP], with only [FIGURE].

Skeleton time-based

Use the line graph skeleton (Section 01). Bar charts plotted across years are read the same way as line graphs: starting position, trend, end position, ranking shifts.

Vocabulary kit

Quantifying groups
outnumbered accounted for made up represented exceeded were equivalent to
Comparison
considerably higher than significantly higher than only marginally above near parity almost double twice as much as
Ranking
the highest the second highest the lowest dominated led the table topped
Concessive markers
albeit in reverse though although despite while
Pitfalls

Don't recite bars in chart order. Group them by logical pattern (similar shapes, similar magnitudes, opposite trends), even if that means jumping around the x-axis. Use respectively aggressively when paired figures appear.

03. Pie chart

Proportional snapshots. Look for the largest and smallest slices, near-equal pairs, and dramatic disparities. For multiple pies, look for shifts between them.

Skeleton single pie

Intro

The pie chart shows the breakdown of [TOPIC] in [PLACE / YEAR].

Overview

Overall, [LARGEST CATEGORY] accounts for the largest share, while [SMALLEST OR NOTABLE OBSERVATION]. Notably, [INTERESTING FEATURE OR DISPARITY].

Body 1: largest segments

In terms of [DIMENSION], [LARGEST CATEGORY] accounts for [PERCENTAGE], followed closely by [SECOND CATEGORY] at [PERCENTAGE]. Together, these two categories make up [TOTAL] of the total. [THIRD CATEGORY] represents a further [PERCENTAGE].

Body 2: smaller segments

At the other end of the scale, [SMALLEST CATEGORIES] account for only [PERCENTAGE_A] and [PERCENTAGE_B] respectively. Notably, [STANDOUT OBSERVATION OR PROPORTIONAL CONTRAST].

Skeleton paired pies

Intro

The two pie charts compare [TOPIC] in [YEAR_A / PLACE_A] and [YEAR_B / PLACE_B].

Overview

Overall, [PRIMARY DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO PIES]. Nevertheless, [SHARED FEATURE OR FORECAST].

Body 1: first pie

Looking at the data for [YEAR_A / PLACE_A] in more detail, [LARGEST CATEGORY] accounted for [PERCENTAGE], while [SECOND CATEGORY] made up another [PERCENTAGE], and [SMALLEST] constituted only [PERCENTAGE].

Body 2: second pie

In contrast, in [YEAR_B / PLACE_B], [CONFIGURATION SUMMARY]. The [CATEGORY] is predicted to [CHANGE], falling from [PERCENTAGE_A] to [PERCENTAGE_B], while [OTHER CATEGORY] will [INVERSE CHANGE]. Conversely, [CONTRAST WITH OTHER PIE].

Vocabulary kit

Proportion verbs
accounted for constituted made up represented comprised was responsible for
Fraction phrases
almost half just over half two-thirds a quarter a fifth a tenth the bulk of the lion's share of
Approximators
approximately around roughly just under just over nearly almost
Magnitude framing
dominated the largest share the bulk of only a mere barely
Forecast hedges
is predicted to is expected to is anticipated is projected to is likely to could
Pitfalls

Don't list every slice mechanically. Group them: largest two, smallest two, the surprising middle. Use percentage, proportion, and share interchangeably to avoid repetition. For paired pies, the overview's job is to capture the most striking difference between them, not just describe each pie separately. Stacked bar charts of proportional data follow the same template.

04. Table

Categorical data in a matrix. Look for the highest and lowest cells, dramatic differences across rows or columns, and year-over-year changes for multi-year tables.

Skeleton

Intro

The table compares [DATA] for [CATEGORIES] in [TIMEFRAME / PLACES].

Overview

Overall, [DOMINANT PATTERN], with [LEADER] [DESCRIPTION], while [CONTRAST]. Notably, [HEADLINE FEATURE OR DISPARITY].

Body 1: the leader or first period

[CATEGORY / PERIOD] [CONTEXT]. [LEADER] dominated with [FIGURE], considerably higher than [NEXT CATEGORY] at [FIGURE]. [FURTHER DETAIL OR COMPARISON], with [CATEGORIES] reaching [FIGURE_A] and [FIGURE_B] respectively.

Body 2: contrasting segment or second period

In contrast, [OTHER GROUP / PERIOD] showed [DIFFERENT PATTERN]. [SPECIFIC FIGURES AND RELATIONSHIPS]. Despite [OBSERVATION], [OUTCOME], reaching a striking [FIGURE] by [YEAR].

Body 3: outlier or smallest figures (optional)

[CATEGORY] was continually [VERB] in relatively small amounts, beginning at only [FIGURE]. Although it did [VERB], reaching a peak of [FIGURE] in [YEAR], this level was short-lived, and by [YEAR_END] it had fallen to [FIGURE].

Vocabulary kit

Dominance
dominated led the table headed the list the leading topped
Comparison ratios
considerably higher than in stark contrast to almost ten times greater than double half five times its 1999 figure
Multi-year change
rose from X to Y fell from X to Y more than doubled halved increased exponentially almost the same as
Highlighting
Notably Interestingly In fact a striking
Pitfalls

Don't recite every cell. Select. Decide whether to group by row (one category across time / place) or by column (one period across categories), and pick whichever makes the comparison cleaner. Respectively is your friend wherever paired figures appear.

05. Process / diagram

Sequential or cyclical stages. Look for the number of stages, time spans, environment shifts, inputs and outputs, and transformations between forms.

Skeleton linear process

Intro

The diagram shows the [PROCESS / STAGES INVOLVED IN] [TOPIC].

Overview

Overall, [the process] comprises [N] main stages, [GENERAL CHARACTERISTIC: time span, environment, key transformation].

Body 1: early stages

The process begins when [INPUT] is [PASSIVE VERB] [HOW / WHERE]. Next, [STAGE 2 DESCRIPTION], where they are [VERB PASSIVE]. The [MATERIAL] is then [VERB PASSIVE], forming [OUTPUT_A], which can now be transported to [NEXT LOCATION].

Body 2: later stages and final output

At [LOCATION / STAGE], the [MATERIAL] is [VERB PASSIVE] by [METHOD]. [MATERIAL] is then [VERB PASSIVE], after which it can be [SEPARATED / COMBINED / USED]. In the final stage, [FINAL OUTPUT] is [VERB] for [PURPOSE].

Skeleton life cycle

Intro

The diagram shows the different stages in the life cycle of [ORGANISM].

Overview

Overall, the cycle takes [TIMEFRAME] and comprises [N] main stages, each of which takes place in [A DIFFERENT ENVIRONMENT / WITH A KEY TRANSFORMATION].

Body 1: early life

The [ORGANISM] begins life as [STAGE 1 FORM], in [ENVIRONMENT], [PROTECTIVE FEATURE / LOCATION]. Over a period of [TIMEFRAME], the [STAGE 1] hatches / develops into [STAGE 2 FORM], which can [NEW ABILITY] and is now known as [NAME].

Body 2: later life and cycle closure

At the end of this stage, the [STAGE 2] [ACTION], where it remains for [TIMEFRAME]. It is in this [ENVIRONMENT] that the [ORGANISM] develops its [CHARACTERISTIC] and reaches its full [SIZE / FORM]. Finally, the adult [ORGANISM] will return to [ORIGINAL ENVIRONMENT] to [REPRODUCTIVE ACT], and the cycle begins again.

Vocabulary kit

Sequential adverbs
First, Next, Then, After this, Following this, At this point, Subsequently, Finally,
Passive transformations
is + [past participle] are + [past participle] has been + [past participle] can now be
Process verbs
undergoes is transformed into develops into hatches into emerges as produces results in gives off yields
Time spans
over a period of [TIME] for [TIME] for the next [TIME] between [TIME_A] and [TIME_B]
Cycle closure
and the cycle begins again completing the cycle returning to its original [ENV]
Structural words
comprises consists of is composed of involves includes
Pitfalls

Use the present simple throughout (is heated, not was heated). Use passive voice for impersonal industrial processes; active is fine for life cycles where the organism is the agent. Include every time span the diagram gives you. Don't invent details that aren't shown. For cycles, close with and the cycle begins again.

06. Map / plan

Spatial change. Look for added structures, removed structures, modified ones, preserved features, and the directional layout. For building plans: rooms, facilities, expansions.

Skeleton two maps, before / after

Intro

The two maps show [PLACE] [BEFORE / AS IT WAS IN YEAR_A] and [AFTER / AS IT IS NOW IN YEAR_B].

Overview

Overall, [PRIMARY CHANGE DIRECTION], with [WHAT IS NEW] and [WHAT WAS REMOVED OR MODIFIED], although [WHAT REMAINED UNCHANGED].

Body 1: before state

Prior to [DEVELOPMENT / CHANGE], this [PLACE] was [GENERAL DESCRIPTION]. In terms of [NATURAL / EXISTING FEATURES], there was [FEATURE] on the [DIRECTION], and [OTHER FEATURE], with [DETAIL]. [ADDITIONAL OBSERVATION about what was absent / what existed].

Body 2: after state

Following [DEVELOPMENT / CONSTRUCTION], although the [PLACE] is now more [DESCRIPTOR], [OVERALL CHARACTER]. The main structures are [KEY ADDITIONS]. [VISITORS / RESIDENTS] can [NEW ACTIVITY], which is [LOCATION]. In terms of access, [TRANSPORT / PATHWAYS], while [OTHER ACCESS DETAIL].

Skeleton building / facility plan

Intro

The two plans show [BUILDING / FACILITY] as it is now and the proposed redevelopment / the new layout following its [CHANGE].

Overview

Overall, while [WHAT WILL BE LOST], the new [FACILITY] will be [LARGER / MODIFIED] and will [NEW CAPABILITY].

Body 1: current state

The current [FACILITY] consists of [GENERAL DESCRIPTION]. The building houses [KEY FEATURES], with a [ROOM] to the [DIRECTION] and a [ROOM] to the [DIRECTION].

Body 2: proposed / new state

Following the [RENOVATION / REDEVELOPMENT], only [WHAT REMAINS] will stay the same. The building will be expanded to the [DIRECTIONS], removing [OLD FEATURES] and making way for [NEW FEATURES]. These include [NEW FACILITY_A] on the [DIRECTION] side, and on the [OTHER DIRECTION], [NEW FACILITY_B]. The [EXISTING FEATURE] will be [MODIFICATION]. On arrival, visitors will benefit from [NEW AMENITIES].

Vocabulary kit

Directional
to the north / south / east / west of on the [SIDE] side of opposite adjacent to alongside in the front to the rear of in the corner
Change verbs (completed)
has been replaced with has been removed has been demolished has been added has been built has been expanded has been widened has been lengthened has been converted into
Change verbs (planned)
will be added will replace is to be built will cater for will be expanded to will be removed
Preservation
remains unchanged has been retained has been preserved is still in place has not been altered
Replacement formula
where there was once [X], there is now [Y]
Style and character
sympathetic to the natural environment single-storey two-storey hut-style untouched relatively simple
Pitfalls

Tense matters. Use present perfect passive for completed changes (has been built); use future for proposed changes (will be built). Always orient the reader with directional language before describing a feature. Don't invent buildings the map doesn't show. Note what stayed the same as well as what changed, this is often half the story.
